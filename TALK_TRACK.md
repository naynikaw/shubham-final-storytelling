# The Geography of Intelligence: 3-Minute Talk Track

**Tone:** Natural, curious, conversational. Like explaining a discovery to a friend.
**Total Time:** ~3 Minutes

---

### 0:00 - The Hook (Hero Section)
*(Start at the top. Let the particles move for a second.)*

"Hi everyone. You know, there's this really popular idea right now that AI is the great equalizer. That because the tools are in the cloud, the builders can be anywhere. We want to believe that the next era of tech is being built in coffee shops in Bali or basements in Berlin."

*(Scroll down to **The Signal in the Noise**)*

"I wanted to believe that too. But when I actually sat down and tracked 940 venture-backed AI startups from the last year, the data told me a completely different story.

It’s not a story about a flat world. It’s a story about a fortress. A really exclusive, really concentrated fortress."

### 0:45 - The Source (The Academic Pipeline)
*(Scroll to **The Academic Pipeline**)*

"It really begins here. We used to talk about the 'college dropout' founder, but this wave feels different. It’s incredibly academic.

Look at this density. Stanford, MIT, Berkeley. These labs are basically the supply chain for the entire industry. It seems like the distance between publishing a paper and launching a company has basically collapsed. If you aren't paying attention to these three campuses, you're missing where the actual signal is coming from."

### 1:15 - The Location (Geographic Concentration)
*(Scroll to **Geographic Concentration**)*

"And the fascinating thing is what happens when those researchers leave the lab. They don't disperse. They cluster.

Almost a third of the entire global AI industry is packed into this tiny 30-mile radius in the Bay Area. Despite all our tools for remote work, it turns out that for this kind of complex, bleeding-edge work, being in the same room still matters. The network effects here are just incredibly powerful."

### 1:45 - The Scale (Global Capital Dominance)
*(Scroll to **Global Capital Dominance**)*

"When you zoom out to the global level, the contrast gets even sharper.

I was surprised by just how dominant the US lead is here—76% of all global funding. The UK is doing great work, but the gap is massive. It really highlights how structural advantages—like deep capital pools and regulatory frameworks—can turn a technology like AI into a massive national export."

### 2:15 - The Shift (Trends & Industry)
*(Scroll to **Temporal Trends**, then **Industry Diversification**)*

"We are seeing a shift in the market, though. The 'easy money' phase of 2024 seems to be cooling off. Capital is down, and the hype is settling.

But that’s actually exciting, because it means the builders are moving on to harder problems. We're seeing this migration from 'Bits' to 'Atoms'—startups applying these models to robotics, machinery, and logistics. The 'Brain' is finally getting a 'Body'."

### 2:45 - The Takeaway (The Founder's Playbook)
*(Scroll to **The Founder's Playbook**)*

"So, for anyone looking to build in this space, the map is actually pretty clear.

The data suggests you need to be close to the research, you probably want to be in the Bay Area, and you should be looking at physical problems, not just software ones.

The hype cycle might be over, but the real building phase is just getting started."

*(End)*
